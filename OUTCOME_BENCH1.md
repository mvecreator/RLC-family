# OUTCOME-BENCH1 — first blind prediction corpus

OUTCOME-BENCH1 is the first fixed benchmark corpus for OUTCOME-BLIND1.

It does not add a new human-behavior mechanism.

Its purpose is to answer:

> Does the current prefix-only RLC projection beat trivial baselines, and under what kinds of future?

## Predictors compared

Three predictors are frozen.

### 1. RLC projection

Uses:

- accumulated state at cutoff;
- recent prefix-only combined load;
- the current PERSON-LOAD / link-strain accumulator equation.

### 2. Persistence baseline

Predicts:

```text
STABLE
```

for every person and link.

### 3. Linear-trend baseline

Fits the recent slope of the accumulated state itself and extends that line across the prediction horizon.

This is a substantially stronger baseline than persistence.

## Direction corpus

The first corpus contains 34 deterministic histories.

### Constant-load continuation

24 cases:

```text
6 load levels
x
4 initial accumulated states
```

### Ramp continuation

4 cases:

- low -> high;
- high -> low;
- moderate rise;
- moderate decline.

### Hidden regime shift

4 cases.

The prefix suggests one regime, but the hidden suffix abruptly changes load after cutoff.

### Trend reversal

2 cases.

The prefix has an explicit rising/falling trend, then the hidden suffix reverses it.

## Reference directional scores

### Person accumulated load

| Predictor | Correct | Total | Accuracy |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| RLC projection | 26 | 34 | 76.47% |
| Persistence | 15 | 34 | 44.12% |
| Linear trend | 17 | 34 | 50.00% |

### Relationship-link strain

| Predictor | Correct | Total | Accuracy |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| RLC projection | 26 | 34 | 76.47% |
| Persistence | 14 | 34 | 41.18% |
| Linear trend | 17 | 34 | 50.00% |

The RLC projection therefore beats both simple baselines on this synthetic corpus.

That result must be interpreted carefully because the continuation cases are generated by the same accumulator family used by the predictor.

It is an internal consistency advantage, not external predictive validation.

## Regime breakdown

### Person direction

| Regime | N | RLC | Persistence | Linear trend |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| constant | 24 | 24 | 15 | 15 |
| ramp continuation | 4 | 2 | 0 | 2 |
| hidden shift | 4 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| trend reversal | 2 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

The key result is not the aggregate 76.47%.

It is:

```text
continuation regime -> often predictable
hidden regime shift -> not predicted
```

The current model predicts dynamics conditional on recent forcing.

It does not predict future external shocks that leave no prefix evidence.

## Localization corpus

A second four-case set asks:

> Which person crosses RECOVERY_ATTENTION first?
> Which link crosses RELATIONSHIP_REPAIR_REVIEW first?

Cases:

1. person-targeted load continues;
2. link-targeted load continues;
3. hidden person-load switch after cutoff;
4. hidden link-load switch after cutoff.

Reference result:

| Predictor | Correct | Total | Accuracy |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| RLC projection | 2 | 4 | 50% |
| Persistence | 0 | 4 | 0% |
| Linear trend | 2 | 4 | 50% |

The two continuation cases are localized correctly.

The two hidden switches are intentionally missed.

## Evaluator defect found by the benchmark

Before OUTCOME-BENCH1, `predicted_first_person_attention` and `predicted_first_link_repair` could select an entity that had already crossed its threshold before cutoff.

The hidden-future evaluator correctly excluded already-crossed entities.

That made the prediction/actual localization contracts asymmetric.

OUTCOME-BENCH1 fixes this:

```text
"first crossing" = first NEW crossing after cutoff
```

for both predictor and evaluator.

## What the benchmark establishes

The current equations have useful conditional continuation behavior.

On this synthetic corpus:

```text
RLC projection > persistence
RLC projection > linear trend
```

for overall direction.

But:

```text
hidden future regime changes -> all current predictors fail
```

and localization is only 50% in the deliberately mixed continuation/switch set.

## What it does not establish

OUTCOME-BENCH1 does not show that the model predicts:

- real relationship breakdown;
- clinical stress;
- real sleep outcomes;
- real family events;
- future external shocks.

The trajectories are synthetic.

The next evidence level requires genuinely independent histories whose hidden future was not used to choose model coefficients.

## Next evidence gate

The next useful step is not another solver feature.

It is:

```text
HISTORY-DATA1
-> preregister cutoffs/horizons
-> freeze model coefficients
-> collect independent dated histories
-> OUTCOME-BLIND1
-> compare against baselines
```

The strongest future result would be reproducible out-of-sample advantage across multiple independent histories.
