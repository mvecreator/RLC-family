# ACOUSTIC-INDUCTION1 — wall transmission and transient acoustic coupling

ACOUSTIC-INDUCTION1 models sound from one household reaching another PERSON2
node through a wall.

The target drive is separated into two terms:

\[
u_{acoustic}(t)=T\,s(t)+M_a\,\dot s(t).
\]

Where:
- \(s(t)\) is an observed or synthetic sound-source waveform;
- \(T\in[0,1]\) is direct wall transmission;
- \(M_a\) has units of days and scales the transient/derivative term.

M_a is an **inductive-like acoustic coupling coefficient**. It is not a
claim that literal electromagnetic mutual induction occurs between people.

## 1. Why two terms

A sustained audible source can matter even when it is not changing:

\[
s(t)=const
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
T s(t)\neq0,\qquad
M_a\dot s(t)=0.
\]

A rapid onset, knock, shout edge, door slam or sudden level transition has:

\[
|\dot s(t)|\gg0
\]

and can therefore create an additional transient term.

~~~text
steady audible background  -> direct transmission
sharp acoustic transition  -> direct + transient derivative coupling
~~~

## 2. Wall coupling

Scenario syntax:

~~~json
{
  "acoustic_induction": {
    "couplings": [
      {
        "id": "wall-family3-central",
        "source_zone": "household2_family3",
        "target_person": "central",
        "transmission": 0.42,
        "derivative_coupling_days": 0.0035
      }
    ]
  }
}
~~~

Poorer acoustic isolation is represented by larger transmission.

The derivative coefficient is independent. A wall may transmit substantial
steady sound without requiring a strong derivative term.

Supported first-gate waveforms:

~~~text

~~~

Pulse and step use smooth edges so \(\dot s\) is finite and explicitly
calculable.

## 4. Household topology

The canonical example reuses the mixed-household topology:

~~~text
central

household1:
  solo

household2:
  husband + wife + adult son

household3:
  husband + wife
~~~

Following the stated geometry, each neighboring household has an acoustic wall
coupling to central.

No acoustic wall coupling between the neighboring households is assumed unless
explicitly declared.

Internal and cross-household social links remain separate from acoustic
couplings.

## 7. H0 / HT / HM / HTM hypotheses

The first calibration compares four models.

### H0

\[
T=0,\qquad M_a=0.
\]

No acoustic coupling.

### HT

\[
T>0,\qquad M_a=0.
\]

Direct wall transmission only.

### HM

\[
T=0,\qquad M_a>0.
\]

Derivative/transient coupling only.

### HTM

\[
T>0,\qquad M_a>0.
\]

Both terms.

A synthetic trace generated by HTM is then fitted by all four hypotheses.

This verifies that the code can distinguish the two mechanisms. It does **not**
establish that real acoustic data require M_a.

## 8. Falsification rule for M_a

The derivative mechanism should be retained only if it improves prediction on
held-out observed waveforms beyond the simpler HT baseline.

The real scientific question is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Does }T s(t)+M_a\dot s(t)
\text{ predict held-out listener response better than }T s(t)?
}
\]

If not:

~~~text
M_a -> remove from empirical model
~~~

The analogy alone is not evidence.

~~~text

~~~

## 11. Interpretation boundary

ACOUSTIC-INDUCTION1 may model:

~~~text
audible sound exists
wall transmits part of it
rapid changes create transient input
listener model state changes
repeated inputs may accumulate
~~~

It does not infer:

~~~text
the sound was directed at the listener
the source intended to upset the listener
the source was hostile
multiple households coordinated
a specific psychological state caused the sound
~~~

Those require independent evidence.
